I closed my final seedling of doomscrolling today: the Facebook website via browser, the last one left standing. 

I don't quite remember how I began this journey driven by my own choice, though it likely started on the MTR. Seeing everyone glued to their phones, brushing away any opportunity to interact, rejecting the world around them—at some point you just think: *come on!* 

So over the past few weeks, I made a rule to commute, wait, and sit without touching the screen unless strictly needed, at most just listening to music. 

Lately, it has felt like my Default Mode Network is finally switching on more often. It’s quite a unique feeling to simply be at ease, fully aware of your surroundings, and most importantly, in the present situation. 

The devastating part is realizing that this has become a "goal" and not "normal."

A while back, I mentioned this practice to a friend, recommending that he try letting himself just sit and wait during transits. He laughed and told me his family would think he had gone completely crazy if he attempted "the art of waiting."

Think about that for a second. 

If you sit quietly on a train, looking at the people around you and entertaining your own thoughts, society assumes there is something fundamentally wrong with you. But if you stare blankly at an illuminated rectangle, tapping your thumb for four hours straight to feed an insatiable urge for stimulus, you are considered perfectly well-adjusted.

We have managed to build an environment where the natural human baseline looks like madness.

Talk about trying to be normal, eh?
