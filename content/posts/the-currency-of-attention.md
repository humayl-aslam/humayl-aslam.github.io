Surveyors are humans too. 

Walking down the street and seeing everyone glued to their phones—brushing away any opportunity to interact, rejecting, and sometimes even frowning at them—it must feel quietly devastating from their point of view. It’s just another human being trying to earn a livelihood within a flawed "corporate charity" system. 

They almost always try to stop me. And whenever I have a few extra minutes, I stop and talk to them. 

I usually don't donate or subscribe to whatever cause they are assigned to pitch. But I’ve come to realize that having an unhurried moment with another person has become a rare commodity in this city. I know it is for me, and I suspect it is for them, too. 

If nothing else, I hope stopping makes their afternoon a little less demoralizing. 

It is an odd feature of modern city life: we spend hours consuming content about human connection on our screens, but when a living, breathing person stands directly in front of us on the pavement asking for two minutes of our day, our first instinct is to scowl and protect our stride. 

Sometimes the kindest thing you can spend on someone isn't money. It's just looking them in the eye and letting them be a person for a minute.
